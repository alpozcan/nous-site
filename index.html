One edition of AI news a day.

Nous AI Newsletter reads the public sources so you do not have to: roughly thirty to fifty ranked items each day, each with a short written summary, plus an executive brief of the day.

iOS · on the App Store

What it does

  • A fixed daily edition

    An edition is frozen when it is published, so it will not shuffle under you during the day. Every item links to the original, and the original is the authority.

  • Ranked, not endless

    Thirty to fifty items, selected and ordered by software rather than by a feed that never ends. Quiet days are genuinely quieter.

  • Public sources only

    RSS and Atom feeds from news outlets, research labs and companies; Hacker News; arXiv; and public posts from a curated list of accounts on X.

  • An executive brief

    A short written summary of the day, so you can read the shape of it in a minute and go deeper only where it matters.

  • Archive, search and Listen

    Nous+ adds past editions, search across them, Listen (the spoken brief), Storylines across the archive, breaking alerts and the Week in AI. The daily edition, the brief, the notification and following are free.

  • No account needed

    Everything works without signing in. The app proves to the server that it is a genuine copy of itself using Apple App Attest. If you want email from Nous, such as the monthly AI News Brief, you can create an optional account with Sign in with Apple, and delete it in the app at any time.

Privacy

No account needed. No advertising. No tracking.

Nous never asks who you are unless you choose to create the optional account, which is only for email. Without it, the server has no way of finding out. It keeps one anonymous identifier per installed copy of the app, and, only if you switch notifications on, the settings a notification needs. Nothing about you is sold or shared for advertising, and no IP address is ever written to the database.

Nous+ purchases are recorded by RevenueCat, under an anonymous identifier, so that we can count trials and subscriptions, and crash reports go to Sentry. Neither is used for advertising or tracking, and you can switch both off in the app's settings. The privacy policy lists everything the server keeps, column by column, and every service that handles anything.
