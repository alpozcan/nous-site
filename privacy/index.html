Nous — Privacy Policy

Nous has no accounts and collects no personal data. This is exactly what the app sends, what the server keeps, and for how long.

Last updated 2026-09-10

The short version. Nous has no accounts. There is nothing to sign up for, no email address to give, and no password. The app does not ask who you are and the server has no way of finding out. It keeps one anonymous identifier per installed copy of the app so that it can tell a real copy of the app from a script, and — only if you switch notifications on — the settings a notification needs. It contains no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party trackers, and nothing about you is sold or shared for advertising.

Who is responsible

Nous is made by Alp Özcan, an individual developer. For anything in this policy, including a request to delete data, write to hi@alp.me. Mail is usually answered within a few days.

There is no account

The app authenticates itself, not you. It uses Apple App Attest: on first launch your device generates a cryptographic key inside its Secure Enclave, and Apple vouches that the key belongs to a genuine, unmodified copy of this app on genuine Apple hardware. Every later request carries a short signature made with that key.

This proves that a request came from the app. It proves nothing about you, and it cannot: the key is created on the device, the private half never leaves it, and no name, email address, phone number or Apple ID is involved at any point.

Only if you turn on notifications

The daily notification is off until you switch it on. When you do, the app registers with the server and it stores: the notification token Apple issues for that install, whether it is a development or production token, your device's language and region setting, your device's time zone, the time of day you chose, whether the notification is on or off, the app's version number, the key identifier above, when the registration was made and last refreshed, which edition was last delivered and when, a count of consecutive delivery failures, and the date the Nous+ subscription proven at registration ends.

Switching notifications off deletes that row immediately — it is a delete, not a flag. If you never switch them off, the row is deleted 180 days after the app last contacted the server.

Subscriptions and payment

Nous+ is sold through Apple's In-App Purchase system: 1 week free, then US$1.99 per month until cancelled. Apple takes the payment. Your name, your Apple ID and your payment details go to Apple and are never sent to this server, which has no way to ask for them.

To decide whether a request is entitled, the app sends the signed receipt Apple gives it. The server checks Apple's signature on that receipt and reads six things from it: the app it was issued for, which subscription was bought, when it expires, whether it was refunded, whether it came from Apple's test environment, and the reference number described next. The receipt itself is then discarded. It is not written to the database, and no transaction identifier, purchase history, price or Apple ID is stored.

The subscription reference number

So that one subscription unlocks one copy of the app rather than being passed around, the app generates a random reference number when you subscribe and hands it to Apple as part of the purchase. It is not derived from anything: not from your Apple ID, not from your device, not from you. Apple echoes it back inside every signed receipt for that subscription, and the server stores it next to the key identifier of the copy of the app that first presented it, along with when that happened, when it was last seen, and how many times it has moved to a different copy.

That is the whole of it: a random number, an anonymous key identifier and three dates. It is deleted when you delete your data, and automatically after a year in which the subscription is never presented.

The one other thing kept is the entitlement's end date, and only if you have notifications on: it is stored on the notification row described above, so that delivery stops when a subscription lapses without your device having to call in.

You can view or cancel the subscription at any time at https://apps.apple.com/account/subscriptions. See the subscription terms for the rest.

IP addresses

Your IP address reaches the server, because that is how the internet delivers a request. It is used for one thing: counting requests per minute so that the free, unauthenticated part of the handshake cannot be abused. The count lives in memory, in one-minute windows, and IPv6 addresses are truncated to their network prefix before being counted. No IP address is written to the database, ever.

Diagnostic logs

The server writes a line per authenticated request containing whether the signature verified, the reason if it did not, the use counter, the path that was requested, and the first eight characters of the key identifier — a prefix, so a log line cannot be tied back to a row on its own. Turning notifications on or off writes a line with the language, time zone and time of day chosen.

Nothing written by this server records a search term: the line records which endpoint was called, never the query. A search does travel to the server inside the web address of the request, so it can appear in the connection records the hosting provider keeps for its own operations, in the same way any website's search does. It is not stored here, not kept in the database, and not associated with an install.

These lines go to the hosting provider's log stream and are kept for as long as that provider keeps them. They are read to diagnose faults and are not analysed for anything else.

What is never collected

No name, email address, phone number, postal address, contacts, photos, calendar, microphone, camera, health or fitness data, or precise or approximate location. No advertising identifier. No fingerprinting. The app contains no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting SDK, no advertising network and no third-party tracker of any kind, and no data leaves the app except the requests to this server described above.

Nothing is sold. Nothing is shared with data brokers. Nothing is used to build a profile or to target advertising, here or anywhere else.

The news itself, and the summaries

Nous reads publicly available sources — RSS and Atom feeds published by news outlets, research labs and companies; Hacker News; arXiv; and public posts from a curated list of accounts on X. It ranks them and writes short summaries using Anthropic's models.

All of that happens on a schedule, on the server, before any device asks for anything. Your reading is not part of it. Nothing you tap, search or open is sent to Anthropic or to any other model provider, and no request from your device causes a model to run. What is sent to Anthropic is the text of published articles, which was already public.

Because the sources are third parties, Nous does not control what they publish. Summaries are generated automatically and can be wrong. If something in an edition is inaccurate, offensive or should not be there, write to hi@alp.me and it will be removed.

Deleting your data

There is no account to delete, but there are rows, and all of them can go — from inside the app, without asking anyone:

  • Delete all my data, in the app's settings, deletes everything described above in one action: the key identifier, its public key and counter, any notification record, and the subscription reference number. It happens immediately, and the app tells you what was deleted. The app then starts fresh with a new, unrelated identifier the next time it is opened. Your subscription is not affected — it belongs to your Apple ID, not to this app — and it will simply attach itself to the new copy.
  • Switching notifications off deletes the notification record on its own, immediately, and leaves the rest.
  • Deleting the app without using either control leaves only the key identifier, which is deleted automatically 180 days later.

If you would rather ask a person, write to hi@alp.me. Because nothing held here identifies you, a mailed request has to name the identifier the app shows in its settings — there is no other way to tell which row is yours, which is a consequence of collecting nothing rather than an obstacle put in the way.

Your rights

If you are in the EU, the UK, or a US state with a privacy statute, you have rights to access, correct, delete and export what is held about you, and to object to its processing. Exercise any of them by writing to hi@alp.me. The practical answer is usually short, because the honest answer to “what do you hold about me” is the two rows described above and nothing else.

The legal basis for holding them, under the GDPR, is legitimate interest: running the service, preventing abuse, and delivering a notification you asked for. There is no automated decision-making about you.

The one thing that is switched on by an explicit choice is the daily notification, and withdrawing that choice is the toggle in the app: switching it off both stops the notification and deletes the record, in one action. Nothing else requires or relies on your consent, so there is nothing else to withdraw — and the rest can still be deleted, as described above.

Children

Nous is a news reader for a professional audience. It is not directed at children, collects nothing that would identify a child, and has no social features.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and this page is the current version. Material changes will also be noted in the app's release notes.
